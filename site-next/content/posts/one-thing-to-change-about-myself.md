# Reflections on Growth — Interview-Style Questions

These are responses I wrote out for self-reflection (also useful for behavioral interview prep). Three questions, three honest answers.

## One Thing I Pushed Myself to Change (努力改变自己的一件事)

It was my experience in a student club during college. I've always been someone who quietly does their own thing — not really a "lead the charge" kind of person. But in my second semester of freshman year, I wanted to stay in 《际遇》杂志社, so I worked up the courage to run for department head.

Even though I didn't think I was ready, the team voted for me. That moment of validation made me realize stepping out of your comfort zone is actually worth it.

## One Thing I'd Do Differently (认为有更好的做法的一件事)

For a long time, I was stubborn — I'd dig in on my own opinion and want to "win" arguments. Coming to the US for school changed that. Team projects are the norm here, and I quickly realized that being inflexible doesn't help anyone.

So I worked on it: listening more, giving thoughtful feedback instead of just pushing back, and doing my homework *before* discussions so that when I did disagree, I could actually back it up with reasoning.

## The Hardest Moment (困难的时刻)

Senior year of undergrad — deciding what comes next. Study abroad, grad school, or get a job? Looking back it seems like just a choice, but at the time it felt like a fork in the road that would define everything.

The worst part was the paralysis at the beginning: trying to chase all three paths at once (保研、找工作、申请国外学校) and ending up nowhere with any of them.

Eventually I got clear on what I actually wanted, committed, and made it happen. The biggest lesson: independent thinking and decision-making matter just as much as the willpower to follow through once you've decided. No matter what you choose, if you can pursue it with a calm and steady mindset, you won't feel like you've wasted your shot at life.
