# 20 Career Development Tips — Notes from a Knowledge Planet

> **TLDR:** A collection of 20 hard-won observations about work, learning, time, relationships, and career. Ranges from tactical (track your time) to philosophical (find your own joy). Worth re-reading periodically.

![stairway to the clouds](/images/stairway-to-the-clouds.png)

These came from a "知识星球" Q&A collection — 780 questions answered in one season. The best insights are below.

---

**1. Learning isn't just absorbing new things — summarizing what you already know counts too.** Finish a book? Write a summary. Finish a project? Capture the method. Rushing to the next thing isn't more productive.

**2. A good retrospective (复盘) finds either a flawed decision model or a critical behavioral error.** Not 40 improvement points in an Excel sheet that nobody reads.

**3. 复盘 can become a form of bureaucracy.** When every project requires a retrospective meeting, it quickly becomes exhausting and hollow. A meeting isn't a management tool — it's a conversation.

**4. Doing the work well is one skill. Extracting and articulating the method is a different skill entirely.** The second one is what lets you scale and teach. A great salesperson who can't explain their process is a ceiling, not a ladder.

**5. A good decision framework: present facts → reason through logic → defer to role if both are equal.** When data can decide, use data. When logic can decide, use logic. When both paths are valid, let the person most accountable for the outcome make the call.

**6. Even a perfect company will see ~10% natural attrition.** If you add unclear growth paths, poor management, or low pay, that number climbs. Retention isn't about blocking exits — it's about investing in hiring and development early.

**7. Standard vs. custom features: when cash is tight, build what's closest to the money. When cash is flowing, build what captures the most market share.** Sometimes generic features win, sometimes customer-specific ones do.

**8. Every job has repetitive work at its core — the content just differs.** Writers write constantly. Scientists run experiments constantly. Developers code constantly. Learning comes from the dirty, repetitive work too.

**9. Don't map a superstar's story onto yourself.** Gates, Jobs, Zuckerberg dropped out of school and succeeded. That's correlated with their extraordinary talent and specific circumstances, not the dropping out itself. For most people: wait for the spark before pouring fuel.

**10. If you suspect your manager is gaslighting (PUA) you, the self-fulfilling prophecy tends to kick in.** Don't overthink it. Focus on doing good work and building a portfolio of achievements — at least something you can put on your resume.

**11. Management of small teams is mostly invisible.** If you're a PM or engineer leading 20 people, you should still be doing the actual work. Pure "people management" at that scale is just overhead. Stay sharp on the craft.

**12. You don't know where your time goes? Track it.** From the moment you wake up, log every hour for a week. You'll quickly see how much actually went to work vs. games vs. scrolling vs. just... spacing out.

**13. "I don't have time" is usually an excuse.** The real thing people are avoiding is saying "I don't prioritize that." Be honest about it.

**14. You might not need marriage, but you probably need a deep relationship.** Marriage is a social construct — a lifestyle choice, not a requirement. When older family members push for it, they're usually projecting a familiar future they can understand onto you. New life models are hard for them to imagine.

**15. Build psychological boundaries with immature parents and elders.** You can listen without letting it in. Be warm, be firm.

**16. Don't grip friendships too tightly.** Some friends drift away — wish them well. Some get closer — cherish them. Some stick around for years — nurture those.

**17. Don't put yourself in grand narratives.** Dreams, frameworks, "bottom-line thinking" — all a bit overrated. Things get built one brick at a time, through patient iteration and attention to detail. Master your craft. Do specific things.

**18. Finding your own joy is a skill.** Some people are chronically unhappy — not because life is hard but because they never learned to be happy. The gap between your ideal life and reality creates fog. If you can find enjoyment inside that fog, life gets easier.

**19. Stay playful outside of work.** The people who seem most relaxed and creative tend to have multiple interests they've sustained for years — writing, reading, photography, sports. The world is full of interesting things; you don't have to search for them.

**20. Don't let "management" become your identity too early.** If you're an engineer or PM who becomes a team lead and stops doing the actual work, you become disposable fast. The ability to lead *and* still do the work is what makes you irreplaceable.
